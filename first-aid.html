🔄Email breached? Change your passwords

Someone may have your email address or password. Work through these steps in order, starting now.

  1. On a device you trust, change the password of the breached account. Use a long, unique one, such as four random words.
  2. Change the password on every other site where you used the same one. Start with banking, other email accounts and shopping.
  3. Sign out of all other devices. In a Google account: Security, then Your devices, then Sign out.
  4. Turn on 2-step verification so a stolen password alone is not enough (see the 2-step verification box).
  5. Check your email settings for forwarding rules, a changed recovery email or a changed phone number. Attackers often add these.
  6. Expect phishing emails now. If card or bank details were involved, call your bank.

🚑Clicked a bad link? Act fast

Speed matters most in the first hour. Do these in order.

  1. Close the page. Do not type anything into it or tap anything else.
  2. If you downloaded a file, do not open it. Delete it and run a scan with Google Play Protect or Windows Security.
  3. If you entered a password, change it now from a different device, then turn on 2-step verification.
  4. If you shared an OTP or card or bank details, call your bank at once and block the card. In India you can also call the cyber fraud helpline 1930.
  5. Report it at the Cyber Crime Portal and to the service that was imitated. Keep screenshots of the message and link.

🔐Turn on 2-step verification

A second step means a stolen or weak password alone cannot open your account. Menus can look a little different on your phone.

  1. Open your Google account, go to Security, then 2-Step Verification, and choose Get started.
  2. Sign in again, then add a second step. An authenticator app or a passkey is safer than text messages.
  3. Save your backup codes somewhere safe that is not your email.
  4. Repeat for your other accounts. On Instagram: Settings, then Accounts Center, then Password and security, then Two-factor authentication.
  5. Use the directory below to find the setting on other sites.

🚨Report the fake profile

Someone is pretending to be you. Collect proof first, then report.

  1. Take screenshots of the fake profile, its username and its link, and of any messages it sent.
  2. Report it in the app. On Instagram: open the profile, tap the three dots, then Report, then choose that it is pretending to be someone.
  3. Tell your friends and followers it is not you, and ask them to report it too.
  4. If it is using your photos to scam people or asks for money, file a complaint at the Cyber Crime Portal (helpline 1930).
  5. Set your own account to private and check who can tag you.

✅Take back app access

Apps and devices you forgot about can still see your data. Remove what you do not need.

  1. On Android, open Settings, then Privacy, then Permission manager. Look at Camera, Microphone, Location and Photos.
  2. Switch off anything an app does not need, or set it to Only while using the app.
  3. On an iPhone, open Settings, then Privacy and Security, and review each permission. Safety Check lets you reset access in one go.
  4. In your Google account, open the Security Checkup and remove third-party apps and devices you no longer use or recognise.
  5. Uninstall apps you do not use.

🧹Strip hidden photo data

Photos can carry your location, the time and your device. Remove it before you post.

  1. iPhone: open the photo, tap Share, then Options, and switch Location off before sending.
  2. Android: in Google Photos open the photo, swipe up for details and remove the location.
  3. Windows: right-click the file, choose Properties, then Details, then Remove Properties and Personal Information.
  4. Mac: open the photo in Preview, choose Tools, then Show Inspector, then the GPS tab, and remove the location info.
  5. Check the result with the Photo Metadata checker in the Security Toolkit. Turn off location tagging in your camera settings.
